A reversal is when a Participant's annual reported emissions exceed the crediting level in any calendar year after TREES Credits have been issued to it (7.1, p.55).

- Risk assessment (7.1.1, p.55): the Participant rates its reversal risk each reporting year with the latest TREES Reversal Risk Rating Tool; that is the total rating unless a reversal was reported initially or in the last five years.
- Buffer contribution (7.1.2, p.55): the total risk rating is the "Buffer%" in Equations 29 and 34; contributions go to a single ART-managed pooled buffer.
- Compensation (7.1.3, pp.55-56): buffer credits are cancelled equal to the lower of the emissions above the crediting level or total credits previously issued. Any deficit versus its own contributions must be replenished by the Participant.
- After a reversal: "a Participant must use the highest Risk Rating possible in Section 7.1.1 (25%) plus a reversal increase of 5%, for a total risk rating of 30%" for five calendar years (p.56). A reversal in the first monitoring report also means 25% for the years before it.
- Exit (7.1.4): remaining contributions are cancelled.

The 5% starting risk is set in the Reversal Risk Rating Tool, not TREES 3.0.
